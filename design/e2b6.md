# Design e2b6: Structural templates

This task delivers the two structural views as HTML: authority delegation, a tree of tasks with their assignees, and task assignment, a table of people with one section per email. Both build on the shared architecture of [`438a`](438a.md): the page, the addresses, the symbols, the folds with the lazy rule, the fold rule, the empty form and the one style sheet. This document adds the two models, their templates and what is particular to them. Three choices shape it:

- **The authority tree is nested `details`, one per task and one per group of children**, as `authority.html` draws it. The tree stands open at every level, since the tree is the view's glance; a node's detail and a closed group of children are lazy parts, so a deep tree costs one summary line per task and no more.
- **The server filters the tree for proposed tasks**, not tablo: the adapter keeps a task that is proposed or has a proposed descendant, so the filtered tree keeps the path from the root. Prototype e2b6 answers this, since tablo's own filter returns the proposed tasks without their ancestors.
- **The assignment page holds every email on one page, each in a `details.detail`**, and a gate group of junctions is a lazy part, so the page stays small where a person holds hundreds of junctions.

## The model

The files: `view_structural.go` (the models), `adapt_authority.go` and `adapt_assignment.go` (the adapters), `templates/authority.html` and `templates/assignment.html`.

### Authority delegation

`AuthorityView` holds the counts, the two filter links, the root `AuthNode` and the deciding commits. An `AuthNode` holds its task, its depth, its assignee and relation, whether it is proposed, the fold of its detail with the `AuthBody`, the fold of its children and the children themselves. [`e2b6/authority.html`](e2b6/authority.html) draws:

| Part of the page | Markup | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| The filter | `p.filter` with two links, the current one with `aria-current="true"` | "all *n* tasks" and "proposed tasks only, *n*"; each is a `Link` with `Swap`, so it is a plain link that HTMX turns into a swap of `main` |
| The off-trunk note | `p.empty` | At a ref off the trunk: every task reads as proposed, and a task whose file differs from the trunk's says so |
| The column header | `p.cols`, `aria-hidden="true"` | "Task" and "Assignee", for the eye; each row names both for a reader who does not see it |
| A node | `li` with `details.detail#t-<id>` | The summary is `span.row`: the id as `code.id`, `span.title`, and `span.who` with the assignee, its relation as `span.sr`, the class `same` where it equals the parent's, the depth class, and `span.proposed` on a proposed task, with "(differs from trunk)" where the file differs |
| A node's detail | part `node`, key the task id | `dl.fields`: assignee and the ancestor it comes from, authorities nearest first with the tasks that make each one, authorisation, a link to the task's definition; for a parent the authorities of its children and a table of the junction defaults it states |
| A node's provenance | `details.prov#prov-<id>`, part `deciding`, key the task id | The deciding commit, the way it accepts, which of author and committer is the authority, how many other tasks it decides, the commands |
| A node's children | `details.kids#k-<id>`, part `kids`, key the task id | A nested `ul` of nodes; the summary reads "*n* children of *id*" |
| The deciding commits | `details.prov#prov-commits`, part `commits` | One row per deciding commit with the tasks it decides, and the count per way of acceptance |

The id in a summary is text, not a link, since a click on a summary opens the fold; the detail holds the link to the task.

The adapter computes these, and nothing else:

- **The tree** from tablo's rows, which stand in display order with a depth each.
- **The relation.** The root reads "the owner". A task whose assignee equals its parent's reads "as the parent" and takes `same`. Any other reads "a delegation". `AuthBody.From` is the parent, and `Delegation` says which of the two wordings applies.
- **The filter.** With the filter on, a node stays when it is proposed or a descendant is; `Root` is nil when nothing stays, and the page shows `p.empty`: "No task is proposed: all *n* are authorised." The `kids` folds of the kept path arrive open whatever the opener says, so the proposed tasks show.
- **The deciding commits.** Rows group by commit, in the order in which display order first meets each commit; `AuthBody.Others` counts the other tasks of the same commit; `Ways` counts in the fixed order of README.md: a change on the trunk, a merge, an `Authorised:` trailer.
- **The wording of a way and of the authority.** `commit`, `merge` and `trailer` read "a change on the trunk by an authority", "a merge by an authority" and "an `Authorised:` trailer"; the authority reads "author", "committer", "author and committer" or "neither". A proposed task reads "Proposed: the deciding commit is not by an authority", and at a ref off the trunk its provenance reads "No commit on the trunk decides this task."

`ByLevel` opens every `kids` fold at every level and every node's detail from detail on. That is VIEWS.md's reading: the indented tree at glance, every chain at detail. The mockup's arrival, where the owner sees the root's detail alone and a parent of leaves folds to a count, is role-specific and belongs to task db74, which needs only its opener to produce it.

### Task assignment

`AssignmentView` holds the glance rows and one `PersonSection` per email in view. [`e2b6/assignment.html`](e2b6/assignment.html) draws:

| Part of the page | Markup | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| People | One table under `h2#people` | A row per email: assigned, contributes next, reviews next, models, or "none: a person"; the row of the marked person is `tr.mine` and reads "(you)" |
| A person | `details.detail#<anchor>` under `h2#carries` | The summary: the email and, in `span.tally`, the kind (owner, agent or person) and the three counts |
| Tasks assigned | `h3` and a table | Task, title, parent, status, and the next gate with the marks of its junction; `details.prov` names the `assignee` field of each task file |
| Contributes, Reviews | `h3` with the totals, then one `details.group` per gate in gate order | The group's summary: the gate, the junctions, how many are next; its body is the lazy part `group` |
| A group | part `group`, key `<anchor>-c-<gate>` or `<anchor>-r-<gate>` | A table under the fold rule: where the person contributes, Task, Title, Model, Reviewer, When; where the person reviews, Task, Title, Contributor, Model, When; then `details.prov` with the entry that supplies each field |
| Counts by gate | `h3` and a table with a `tfoot` | Per gate: the tasks standing there, the junctions contributed and reviewed, and of each how many are next |
| Authority | `h3` and a list | Each subtree the person has authority over, with its count of descendants |

The adapter computes:

- **The anchor** of a person: `p-` and the email in lower case with every character outside `a` to `z` and `0` to `9` replaced by `-`; a second person with the same anchor takes the suffix `-2`. The glance row links to `#<anchor>` when the section stands on the page and to the page of that person otherwise.
- **The kind.** "owner" for the assignee of the root, "agent" for an email with a model, "person" otherwise.
- **`When`**: "next" for the junction at the task's next applicable gate, "passed" at or before the status gate, "later" beyond, in the words of `assignment.html`.
- **The groups.** Junctions group by gate in gate order; inside a group rows are alike, for the fold rule, when model, contributor, reviewer and `When` agree, and the fold's summary names the gate.
- **The counts**, as sums over the lists.
- **The provenance of a group**: one row per distinct pair of value and supplying task among the group's junctions, so a group whose junctions all resolve from one ancestor shows one row per field.

With the `person` parameter the page holds that person's section alone, and the People table the people in view. An email the project does not know yields an empty model: the table gives way to `p.empty`, "No task names an email at this ref.", and no section follows. The status code for an unknown email is the server's (A2).

`ByLevel` opens each person's section from detail on and never opens a group, so at detail a reader sees every group's gate and counts and opens the ones that matter. The mockup's arrival, each group that holds a next junction unfolded and an assignee's own section alone, belongs to db74.

### What the design keeps and drops from prototype e2b6

It keeps: the filter in the server with the path to the root; the filter as a plain link; the wording of the way and of the authority; the honest page for an unknown email; a tree of any depth through one recursive template. It drops: the fragment route that swaps a node's `li` and tracks the open set through `HX-Current-URL`, which native `details` with lazy parts replace, and with it the finding that a dropped header breaks the state; `mode=full`, which `Env.Inline` replaces; one page per email with an index, which the single page with sections replaces, as the mockup draws it. The prototype's finding that native `details` cannot keep its state in the address stands: the address carries the level, the filter and the person, and the folds a reader opens by hand are not in it. Task db74 owns whether an address names open nodes; `Target.Open` carries them when it does.

## Conformance to the texts

| View | VIEWS.md | Golden reference | glance | detail | provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authority delegation | [Authority delegation](https://github.com/nbyoung/tableaux/blob/main/VIEWS.md#authority-delegation) | `tableaux/docs/mockups/authority.html` | The tree: every summary row, with the assignee, the `same` tint and the proposed mark | Each node's `details.detail`: the chain and the junction defaults | Each node's `details.prov`, and the deciding commits |
| Task assignment | [Task assignment](https://github.com/nbyoung/tableaux/blob/main/VIEWS.md#task-assignment) | `tableaux/docs/mockups/assignment.html` | The People table | Each person's `details.detail`: the tasks, the groups by gate at every gate, the counts, the authority | The `details.prov` under the tasks and inside each group |

The assignment view takes no window: it lists every gate, as the owner ruled.

Where a page departs from its mockup:

- **Narration and illustration.** Neither page renders the mockup's explaining lists and paragraphs, the weather-station illustration of `authority.html`, or the notes under the People table of `assignment.html` (the tasks whose next junction is recursive, the agent's self-reviews), which are sentences composed by hand over the lists the page already shows.
- **The proposed filter.** `authority.html` shows the filter's result in a section of its own because it is one static file; the page serves the filtered tree in place of the whole one, as the mockup's comment says the daemon does.
- **Recursive junctions.** `assignment.html` adds, per person, a table of the tasks whose next junction is recursive and their pins. The page shows such a task in Tasks assigned with the mark `🪆` at its next gate; the pin stands on the task's own page.
- **Depth.** The mockup indents the assignee by an inline `style`; the page uses the class `d0` to `d8` (438a, finding C4).
- **Ids in the tree** are text in both; every other task id on the two pages links to its task, where `authority.html` links none.

## Decisions at review

The owner decided each question this design raised on 2026-10-06, together with the cross-design rulings of that review; a decision the review did not reach stands as the design states it, accepted with the design.

1. **One page for every person.** Accepted with the design, as stated: the assignment view is one page with a section per email, as `assignment.html` draws it, and `person` narrows it to one section. The alternative is prototype e2b6's index with a page per email, which keeps each page smaller and costs a click per person.
2. **The tree's arrival without db74.** Accepted with the design, as stated: until task db74 lands its opener, the tree arrives fully unfolded at every level and every node's detail arrives open from detail on. The alternative builds the mockup's arrival, the root's detail alone and a parent of leaves folded to a count, into this task.
3. **Recursive junctions in the assignment.** Accepted with the design, as stated: the page marks them in Tasks assigned and draws no table of pins. The alternative keeps the mockup's table per person, which needs each pin from tablo's assignment view.
4. **The filter's place.** Accepted with the design, as stated: the server filters the full tree. The alternative asks tablo's `493e` to keep and mark the ancestors under its own filter, which saves the full view on a filtered page and binds tabloio's `c48a` to the same data.
5. **The shared rulings.** Decided by the owner on 2026-10-06, through 438a: the self-review mark in its general form, the contributor's mark alone where the reviewer is the contributor (438a decision 2); a long list folds from more than eight alike rows (438a decision 5); and an absent `person` is nobody on the authority and assignment views, so they show everyone (5ca9 decision 3).
6. **The reconciliation with the server as built.** Decided by the owner on 2026-10-07, as proposed, with each of the nine questions of the mapping answered by the choice it makes: [`438a/server.md`](438a/server.md) maps the four template tasks onto the Server as `main` holds it. For this design: the two view files land as `templates/views/authority.html` and `templates/views/assignment.html` and define `view` and `context` beside `body`; a part is `part/NAME`, asked as `part=NAME:KEY`; the filter's links are `Control` values from `Page.Self` and carry `hx-boost="true"` in place of the four swap attributes (T5); the models, the fixtures and T1 to T3, T5, T6, T8 and T9 land first and the routes serve the placeholder until the adapters land with tablo's types, with the views' `Parts` and T4, T7 and T10 to T12; and `source.Request.Proposed` goes, since the adapter filters (decision 4). An email the project does not know answers `200` with the empty form, which settles A2.
7. **The authority data, from the Derivation.** Recorded on 2026-10-07 from tablo's `27a3`, which the owner accepts that day. A3 and A5 are amended: the Derivation gives `Differs`, `Way`, and `Author` and `Committer`; on the trunk a proposed task has a deciding commit and no authority, and off it it has none. The outgoing owner accepts a hand-over of the root (`27a3` decision 3), and on the trunk means on its first-parent line (decision 4).
